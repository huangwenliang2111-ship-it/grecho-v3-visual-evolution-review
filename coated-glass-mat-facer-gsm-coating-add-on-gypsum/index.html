Direct answer. Two coated fiberglass mats with the same quoted total basis weight do not necessarily have the same reinforcement substrate, coating mass, surface openness or behaviour during gypsum-board manufacture. For a meaningful supplier comparison, distinguish base mat mass, dry coating add-on, total finished mass, and the specific side and method of application. Then compare the facer-to-core contact and the resulting board using evidence applicable to each object. A single total gsm figure cannot establish interchangeability.

The purchasing problem behind one number

A gypsum-board manufacturer may receive two offers both described as “200 gsm coated glass mat”. The commercial headline looks identical. Yet one product could allocate more mass to the underlying nonwoven and less to the surface coating, while another uses a lighter nonwoven with a higher coating load. Those are illustrative alternatives, not measurements of actual GRECHO products. They explain why a purchasing decision based only on nominal mass is incomplete.

Johns Manville publicly lists area weight, color and mineral coatings among the adjustable characteristics of fiberglass mat facers for gypsum boards. The same supplier names gypsum-core bonding and control of slurry penetration as relevant performance criteria. That is evidence that these variables matter commercially; it is not a transferable performance guarantee for any other supplier’s material.

What the four terms should mean in an RFQ

Base mat mass identifies the fibrous web before the specified coating stage. Dry coating add-on describes coating solids retained on a defined area after a stated application and drying procedure. Total finished mass includes all relevant layers counted by the supplier’s definition. Coating distribution describes where the material is deposited, which can matter even if the mass balance is identical. A buyer should ask whether each value is nominal, typical or a contractual acceptance limit, and whether the basis is conditioned or dry.

The relationship finished mass = base mat mass + dry add-on is a useful simplified material-balance model for a single coating operation with no losses or other added layers. It is not a universal equation for every multistage product: binder, additional backing, primer, conditioning and analytical method can change what is included. Do not infer coating add-on by subtracting two unrelated product datasheet numbers without shared definitions and methods.

Why a standard mass test is not a substitute for coating identification

ASTM D3776/D3776M-20(2025) describes methods of measuring fabric mass per unit area, with full-piece, full-width and swatch options. The standard’s scope does not by itself separate nonwoven mass from coating add-on or define gypsum-board suitability. Its use must be selected appropriately for the sample and product. In the absence of a validated coating-specific method, buyers should request the supplier’s documented internal specification and sample-based evidence rather than invent a standard-derived percentage.

It is also important to ask for sample identity. A measurement from a small coupon may not automatically establish a full delivery lot’s uniformity. The sampling plan, location across width, conditioning and sampling period should be transparent.

Loose mat, board interface and finished board are separate evidence objects

A coated facer can be weighed as a loose material, examined at its surface, and laminated or embedded into gypsum slurry. A finished glass-mat gypsum board involves additional manufacturing variables: core formulation, wetting, heat/drying history and edge formation. In Europe, EN 15283-1:2008+A1:2009 concerns gypsum boards with mat reinforcement and specifies a product-level scope. The fact that an input roll conforms to a supplier’s gsm tolerance does not by itself show that a changed finished board retains its declared reaction to fire, flexural characteristics or moisture performance.

For an initial replacement discussion, list the existing board declaration and the change relative to the approved input. An altered coating chemistry or contact-side finish may need interface review even when gsm is unchanged. Conversely, a different gsm alone does not establish which particular test will fail. The technical owner should determine the relevant risk and verification scope.

A practical comparison sequence

First, freeze the reference: record the current facer code, sampling date, target application, nominal basis weight and actual material certificate. Second, separate the construction: request base mat and coating definitions, face orientation and where the dry mass is reported. Third, compare the appropriate samples: establish like-for-like measurements and a controlled board trial where justified. Fourth, decide by evidence: record which properties have been demonstrated, which remain supplier statements, and which need finished-board confirmation.

Avoid presenting a universal “ideal ratio” of glass and mineral coating. The useful split depends on the product design and the manufacturer’s board process; there is no evidence in the cited sources supporting a blanket GRECHO recommendation.

FAQ

Is 200 gsm coated glass mat identical to another 200 gsm coated glass mat?

No. Total basis weight does not identify the base web, the coating add-on, coating distribution or core-contact behaviour.

Can a supplier’s coating add-on be calculated from a finished gsm figure alone?

Not reliably. Additional definitions, manufacturing-stage data and a consistent method are required.

Does ASTM D3776 measure adhesion to gypsum slurry?

No. Its purpose concerns fabric mass per unit area; board-interface behaviour requires separate evidence.

Does a different coating automatically change the completed board’s fire class?

That cannot be asserted without understanding the change and the relevant product-level declaration or classification. It requires scope review by the responsible party.

What is the minimum information to send for a sample review?

Current facer and board use, base and finished mass definitions, contact side, coating type if available, relevant process restrictions and existing reports.
