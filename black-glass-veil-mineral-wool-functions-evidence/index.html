Black Glass Veil on Mineral Wool: Appearance, Retention and Wind Protection

Black glass veil appearance, anti-shedding and wind protection are distinct questions. Identify the mineral-wool assembly, claimed function and matching evidence before sampling.

A black glass veil on a mineral wool board may help control what is visible behind a facing, but its colour does not prove wind protection, particle retention or weather resistance. These are different functions, and the appropriate evidence depends on the board construction and intended exposure.

For an insulation-board manufacturer or technical buyer, this distinction matters before selecting a facing sample. An open-joint ventilated façade, a perforated metal acoustic assembly and a temporarily exposed board may all contain a dark surface, but they do not present identical design questions. This guide concerns the role of a facer on mineral wool, not the approval of a completed façade or roofing system.

Start with the application, not the colour of the veil

In a ventilated façade with open cladding joints, a black backing surface can reduce the visibility of insulation behind the outer cladding. KS West describes black glass-veil facings for this appearance function in its discussion of ventilated external walls. It also describes a possible glass-veil facing as additional temporary weather protection during construction for the specified context. Those statements concern the described assembly: they are not a general windproof or unlimited-weathering claim for an unidentified black veil. [1]

Before discussing performance, identify which board face is visible, the cladding format and viewing conditions. A sample may help judge visual contrast in that application. It does not by itself establish the continuity or protective capacity of a weather barrier behind open joints.

An acoustic assembly behind perforated metal asks a different question. The material may need a certain appearance at the perforations, and the construction may also describe a function limiting material fallout. These functions must be named separately rather than grouped under the vague expression “black protective tissue.”

Appearance screening and material retention are different tasks

Appearance screening is an optical question: what can be seen through openings and against which background? State the exposed side, surface colour, construction and viewing conditions. An attractive dark finish is not an emission or retention certificate.

Material retention asks what movement or release of insulation material is intended to be limited and at what location. The German term Rieselschutz is often used for a described anti-shedding function in a particular product or assembly. On its German page for the RAF-SE V acoustic infill product, ROCKWOOL identifies black glass veil facing and states an anti-shedding and optical role when positioned toward perforated trapezoidal steel sheeting. This is a named finished-product example, not proof that every black glass veil has the same retention capability. [2]

Nor does the term Rieselschutz establish zero airborne fibre release. A statement about visible material fallout and a quantitative airborne-emission claim are different questions requiring different matching evidence. Keep them separate even if the same surface is involved.

Wind protection belongs to a defined layer and construction

For an open-joint rainscreen façade, the question may be which layer limits wind exposure at the insulation and how that layer works with its joints and penetrations. This cannot be answered from veil colour, a loose sample or the word “facer.”

illbruck’s manufacturer explanation of guidance for ventilated façades distinguishes wind tightness from the building’s air-tightness function and discusses circumstances in which a separate façade membrane or additional protection is considered. It is an explanatory manufacturer article, not a substitute for the complete underlying design guideline or the project specification. [3]

It would be equally unsafe to insist that every project needs an additional membrane, or to declare that the factory-applied black facing removes the need for one. The responsible façade and board teams must identify the required function, the layers that are intended to provide it and the available evidence. A GRECHO facer direction is not a design approval for joints, fixings or the completed rainscreen.

Temporary construction exposure is not permanent weather approval

A facing described as supporting protection during a construction phase should be evaluated for that specified phase. It does not follow that the exposed board may remain outdoors without a time limit or that the surface provides the required long-term rain, moisture or ultraviolet resistance.

The material review should identify the named product, the relevant exposure state, the responsible document and any limits stated by the actual board manufacturer. If those details are unavailable, record the question as open. A photograph of a visually intact board after a short exposure does not establish its longer-term service condition.

Separate the requested functions before selecting a sample

Four different requests that should not be conflated
Requested function Object and evidence to identify What must not be inferred
Visual screening Visible board side, background, cladding openings, colour and viewing condition Windproofing, zero fibre release or waterproofing from a black surface
Limiting material fallout Named faced board, relevant surface or opening, described use and matching product information Universal retention rate or an airborne-emission guarantee from the term Rieselschutz
Wind exposure protection Specified functional layer and its relationship to the complete façade, including joints A complete wind-tight assembly from an isolated veil specimen
Construction-phase protection Specific product documentation and the stated temporary exposure conditions Unrestricted permanent weathering or approval to omit another protective component

The table is a discussion aid, not a construction code or a standardised test matrix. Its purpose is to prevent a material enquiry from turning a visual requirement into an undocumented system-performance promise.

Which evidence can support each statement?

Start with the manufacturer’s description of a named product. It can show what function is claimed for a particular assembly, but it must not be silently extended to an unrelated facer. The ROCKWOOL example above belongs to a specified acoustic roofing product, not to all mineral-wool façade boards.

A sample photograph or controlled appearance review can show the surface and viewing setup, provided the specimen and light/background conditions are recorded. It cannot replace a report on material release or wind protection. For those claims, identify the test object, method, limits and approval context that actually apply. A bare material datasheet and a test of the completed board may concern different specimens.

Where source documents are available only as public descriptions, do not reconstruct a full manufacturer’s test or national standard. Ask the board manufacturer or responsible technical team which decision remains open and which evidence it requires.

Illustrative example: two requests for the same black appearance

Hypothetical example, not a GRECHO customer case: Team A needs a dark facing behind open joints in a ventilated façade and wants to know what remains visible. Team B is reviewing a mineral-wool acoustic product behind perforated steel and is concerned about visible fallout at openings. Both teams might request “black glass veil,” yet they are not asking for the same verification.

Team A should specify its façade build-up and the optical question, then separately assign weather and wind-protection requirements to the responsible system parties. Team B should specify the board, perforated facing, inspected surface and the particular retention claim it needs to investigate. Neither enquiry is resolved by matching colour alone.

Frequently asked questions

Does black glass veil make a mineral-wool board windproof?

No such conclusion follows from its colour. The relevant wind-protection requirement must be assigned to a specified layer and complete construction, with the applicable evidence.

Is Rieselschutz the same as zero fibre emissions?

No. A described anti-shedding role in one assembly is not a universal quantitative guarantee of no airborne fibre release. The claim and evidence must match the actual object and use condition.

Can the same facing be used behind perforated metal and in open-joint cladding?

Do not assume interchangeability. The applications may have different exposure, visual, retention and system requirements. Compare the exact material/board combinations and their supporting documentation.

Does an optical black surface replace a façade membrane?

Not by itself. Whether a membrane is required, and which layer provides a specified function, is a project and system decision that cannot be approved by colour or a loose-facer sample.

What should I send when requesting a mineral-wool facer sample?

Share the board application, exposed side, desired appearance, relevant construction, additional functional question and available documentation. Keep commercial sample discussion separate from the system-level approval process.

References and limits

  1. KS West – ventilated external walls, thermal-insulation discussion. Used for the specific optical-screening and construction-phase description; no universal weathering performance inferred.
  2. ROCKWOOL Germany – RAF-SE V product and application description. A named product example for black veil, anti-shedding and appearance behind perforated metal; not a GRECHO product.
  3. illbruck – manufacturer’s explanatory article on FVHF guidance. Used to distinguish façade protection and wind-/air-tightness concepts; full underlying guideline not reviewed.
  4. GRECHO – German open-joint cladding interpretation, accepted INS-054; paired with the separate Rieselschutz guide, accepted INS-063. They are prior language-specific source owners, not proof of this page’s publication or market demand.

This is an editorial learning guide. It contains no GRECHO material release measurements, product-specific windproofing approval, installation design or customer case.
