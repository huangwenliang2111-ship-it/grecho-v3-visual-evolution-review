Glass-Fleece Facings on PIR, Phenolic and Roof Panels: Identify the Product Before Comparing Evidence

Compare PIR, phenolic and roof-panel glass-fleece descriptions by product type, facing position and evidence scope before requesting a material review.

Direct answer: the same facing term does not identify the same purchased product

No. “Glass fleece”, “glass veil” or “fibreglass facer” does not, by itself, identify the board being purchased or the evidence needed to assess it. The term may describe a thin layer attached to a rigid PIR roofing insulation board, both faces of a phenolic (PF/resol) ceiling-insulation board, or an exterior surface within a factory-made roof panel whose other face is steel. It may also describe a separate roll of facing material not yet incorporated into any board.

For a meaningful technical or commercial comparison, identify the supplied object first: the full product reference, core family, layer sequence, orientation, adjacent interface and the stage at which the relevant claim is being made. Only then decide whether the document in hand is for the loose facing, the factory-made board or panel, or the installed roof or ceiling assembly.

This guide compares publicly described third-party constructions to explain that decision. It is not a product comparison, substitute approval, certification statement or evidence that GRECHO supplies any of the named boards.

First identify what the buyer is actually ordering

Four descriptions can contain the words “glass fleece” while referring to materially different procurement objects:

A separate facing roll. The order concerns a specified nonwoven, coated veil or composite facing before it is combined with foam, an adhesive, a metal skin or a membrane. A facing supplier can describe the identified material and provide relevant material-level documents; those documents alone do not prove a finished board's performance.

A factory-faced rigid insulation board. The order concerns a particular PU/PIR/PUR or PF/resol board, including its core and attached face or faces. A board-level declaration belongs to the defined board variant, not automatically to a replacement facing or a different core.

A factory-made roof panel or component. The order concerns a manufacturer-defined multi-layer component that can combine a foam core, a glass-fleece outer layer and a metal inner skin. Calling one outer layer “glass fleece” does not turn the whole factory-made component into a standalone facing specification.

An installed roof or ceiling assembly. The decision now includes additional layers, attachments, junctions, membranes and conditions outside the delivered factory product. Claims about the assembled waterproofing or other system properties must be supported by evidence for that assembly, not inferred from a single constituent material.

The practical question is not “Do both suppliers offer glass fleece?” It is “Which object, face and interface does each quoted line and test document describe?”

Three construction examples that must not be grouped together

1. PIR roofing insulation: one core, two differently described faces

Kingspan France describes its PIR BV board as rigid polyisocyanurate (PIR) foam with a bitumen-treated glass veil on the upper face and a mineralised glass veil on the lower face. The two surfaces are not presented as one identical facing specification. Manufacturer product description.

When comparing a potential facing material with a description like this, first ask which of those two faces is the subject of the proposal. Then identify the face's adjoining material and the specified board variant. A superficial match in colour, base fibre or area weight would not establish equivalence of the treated surfaces or the board's declared properties.

The publicly available product description does not establish that a particular GRECHO facer matches either PIR BV face, or that one of those surfaces can simply be transferred to another PIR build. For a deeper French-language discussion of the two treatments, see PIR roofing-board facing comparison.

2. PF/resol ceiling insulation: “glass fleece on both sides” is not a PIR specification

Kingspan Belgium describes Kooltherm K10 Plafondplaat as a rigid resol/phenolic foam insulation board with a glass-fleece facing on both faces, intended for such applications as crawl spaces, basement ceilings and parking-garage ceilings. Manufacturer product description.

The word glasvlies on both faces does not tell the purchaser whether both faces have the same coating, manufacturing revision or role in the finished product. Those details require the exact variant's technical documents. More importantly, PF/resol and PIR are different core families; one cannot move a PIR-board test conclusion to a PF board simply because both descriptions mention glass fleece.

A buyer comparing a facing change to a product of this kind should identify the original PF board and its documented configuration before asking whether existing finished-board statements remain applicable. The more detailed Belgian Dutch discussion is available in glass-fleece changes on resol ceiling boards.

3. X-Dek roof component: an exterior glass-fleece layer with an interior steel skin

For the Polish KS X-Dek XG variant, Kingspan describes a factory-made roof panel using an IPN core, an exterior glass-fleece layer and an interior galvanized-steel sheet. The product description also says that an additional PVC waterproof membrane is required after the panel is installed. Manufacturer XG IPN page.

This is a different purchased object again. A quotation for the outer glass-fleece material, for the factory-made XG panel, and for the installed waterproofed roof addresses three different scopes. The site also lists XM variants with an exterior PVC membrane and XD variants with steel on both faces. Those are named variants, not interchangeable descriptions of XG. Manufacturer roof-panel family.

Do not assume the proprietary IPN core is a specified PIR or PF formulation without the corresponding manufacturer's declaration. Similarly, the post-installation waterproof membrane must not be represented as a factory-attached glass-fleece property.

A four-question route before requesting quotations or technical evidence

Rather than comparing all PDFs in one table, route each incoming specification through four questions. The aim is to prevent a category error before a purchase, sample or document decision.

1 — What is the delivered item? Is the request for loose glass-fibre facing, a complete factory-faced insulation board, a factory-made roof panel or an installed system? If two quotations name different objects, they cannot be treated as equivalent quotations yet.

2 — Which exact core and product variant? Record the manufacturer's published name and the stated core family. Keep PIR/PUR, PF/resol, and IPN or QuadCore-branded constructions distinct unless an original declaration explicitly establishes further detail. Do not substitute a generic “foam insulation” label for the product identity.

3 — Which face and next interface? Determine whether a statement relates to the upper, lower, exterior or interior side, and what will contact that face in the described configuration. For XG, distinguish its exterior glass layer from the subsequent PVC waterproofing membrane. Identical names on opposite faces do not resolve which face a proposed material is intended to replace.

4 — Who owns the required evidence? If the question is about the loose veil specification, use the material supplier's matching document. If it concerns board properties, obtain evidence from the board manufacturer for the defined build. If it concerns roof waterproofing or installed performance, obtain the relevant system or project documentation from the responsible party. Do not ask a loose-facer supplier to certify an entire roof construction.

Outcome: after these four questions, the purchaser can say precisely what is to be compared and which document owner must answer the remaining question. Detailed test plans and qualification records should be handled within the relevant product or project workflow, not invented from this identification guide.

Which standards and declarations can be associated with which object?

The standard printed on a technical sheet is only useful once its scope and stated test object are identified.

EN 13165:2012+A2:2016 concerns factory-made rigid polyurethane (PU) insulation products for buildings, with or without facings or coatings; the published scope expressly includes PIR and PUR. It describes products, not the performance of complete systems incorporating them. DIN Media scope.

EN 13166:2012+A2:2016 concerns factory-made phenolic-foam (PF) insulation products, including boards and laminates with or without facings or coatings. Again, product-scope evidence does not by itself demonstrate the performance of a complete construction system. DIN Media scope.

EN 14509:2013 specifies factory-made self-supporting double-skin metal-faced insulating sandwich panels. The published KS X-Dek XG page cites EN 14509:2013 against a particular stated U-value parameter, yet describes an exterior glass-fleece layer and an interior steel skin. That reference alone does not prove that the whole XG construction belongs to the double-metal-faced scope, or certify its loose glass-fleece layer, a replacement material or the completed waterproofed roof. The appropriate product declaration, method and assessed configuration must be confirmed with the manufacturer for the exact variant and claim. DIN Media scope · XG manufacturer description.

The referenced public standard summaries identify product scopes. This guide has not reviewed full paid standard texts or the complete conformity documentation of the named third-party products, and it makes no regulatory determination for an individual project.

What to send when requesting facing-direction support

When a board manufacturer contacts a facing solution partner, a useful description of the request is more valuable than a broad claim such as “equivalent glass fleece required.” State the complete board or panel family, the specific face, the current facing description, the next contacting layer, and whether the question is about a material comparison or an existing product declaration. If a public document does not establish a property or construction detail, mark it not confirmed rather than guessing.

GRECHO can help review application-matched fiberglass facing directions and coordinate selected samples or technical documentation where applicable. Frequently asked questions

1. Does “glass fleece” mean the same product in a PIR board and an X-Dek roof panel?

No. The words describe a material layer, not the complete delivered product. A PIR board with identified upper and lower facers and an X-Dek XG roof panel with exterior glass fleece and interior steel are different manufacturer-defined constructions. Identify the product reference and all faces before comparing specifications.

2. If both faces of a board are called glass fleece, can one facing be used on either side?

Not on that description alone. Both surfaces may share a broad material name without being established as the same treatment or version. Confirm the exact two-face construction and adjoining interface before asking whether a material change is appropriate.

3. Can a phenolic (PF/resol) ceiling board be assessed using a PIR roofing-board declaration?

Not simply because both contain glass-fibre facings. PF/resol and PU/PIR belong to different product families. Their relevant product declarations, intended applications and tested constructions must be identified separately.

4. Does a manufacturer's reference to EN 14509 prove that an exterior glass-fleece layer is certified?

No. EN 14509:2013 has a defined double-metal-faced sandwich-panel scope. A manufacturer may cite a method or parameter in its product information, but that alone does not certify a standalone glass-fleece facing, establish the whole product's standards applicability or validate an installed roof.

5. Is a facer datasheet enough to claim waterproofing or other installed-system performance?

No. A loose-facing document addresses the identified material within its stated method and scope. Waterproofing after installation involves the specified membrane, joints and full assembly, and needs the relevant system or project evidence. A facing supplier's document cannot replace that evidence.
